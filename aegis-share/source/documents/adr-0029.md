# 0029. A building's kit of parts is drawn from the model, not stored in it

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-08-06

## Context

Every building is drawn as one unit box scaled to its OBB and its height. That
holds while buildings are boxes and stops holding the moment they are authored
meshes, because the instance matrix scales width, height and depth independently.

Measured on akiba-01 at 2048 m and 512 cells, `heightM / min(w, d)` at p50/p90:
slumShack 0.99/1.71, detachedHouse 0.77/1.25, luxuryResidence 0.99/1.48, casino
2.53/5.77, corpoTower 7.82/22.62, megabuilding 45.20. Footprint aspect, long side
over short, stays inside 1.06–2.58 across p10–p90 for every archetype. So the
horizontal stretch is mild everywhere and one authored mesh survives it, while the
vertical stretch destroys any repeated detail — and only on the towers. That is
what makes a kit of parts the answer for those rather than a single mesh, and it
is why `corpoTower` is the archetype the kit is proved on first.

Where the expansion of one building into its parts lives is the decision. The
tempting home is `assemble.ts`, whose own comment says the renderer is a dumb
consumer that uploads a buffer, and which already turns one megabuilding into a
variable number of stacked tier instances — 2 to 4, from `BUILDINGS.megabuilding`'s
`minTiers` and `maxExtraTiers`.

## Decision

Kit expansion is **renderer-side**, under
`src/components/features/city-map/viewer/kit/`: a manifest naming the parts and
their assets, a pure expander from `Building[]` plus part metrics to per-part
instance buffers, and an uploader that owns the THREE objects. `assemble.ts`,
`CityModel`, `entities/city`'s vocabulary and every golden hash are untouched.

ADR-0028 rejected viewer-only grading because `baseZM` is a model value: the
`buildings` stage reads it, so two authorities over one quantity would drift apart.
A part breakdown has no second consumer — no stage reads it, and the same
`CityModel` drawn with a kit or with a box is the same city by the definition the
product uses, `contentHash`. The line already drawn here is massing against dress.
Megabuilding tiers are model values because they change the volume the city
occupies; kit parts tile a volume already decided. The storey is likewise already
a drawing unit rather than a city one — `FLOOR_PITCH_M = 3.6` lives in the
viewer's `windowMaterial.ts` — and a floor module is that constant in mesh space.

Height keeps one authority. The expander takes
`floors = max(1, round((heightM - baseH - crownH) / floorH))` and then scales the
floor stack so the assembly ends at exactly `heightM`. Part metrics are measured
off the loaded meshes rather than written into the manifest, so they cannot drift
from what Blender exported.

## Alternatives considered

- **Expand in `assemble.ts`.** Rejected on a cost that recurs rather than on
  purity: the engine would take part heights as input, so `generateCity` becomes a
  function of asset files, and re-authoring a crown in Blender moves the
  `buildings` hash and `contentHash` on every seed. `golden.ts` says a diff there
  means the generator's output changed, "which is either the point of your change
  or a bug" — an art iteration is neither. `entities/city` already keeps
  `discards` outside `contentHash` on the same reasoning: what the engine reports
  is not what the city is.
- **A `src/lib/citykit/` layer.** Rejected: the expander exists to feed this
  viewer's instanced meshes and is parameterised by this kit's assets, so it fails
  the test ADR-0027 used to admit the engine into `src/lib/**` — a web of pure
  functions with zero component affinity that could ship as a library. Its
  substance survives as module structure inside `viewer/kit/`.
- **One stretched mesh per archetype.** Rejected for the towers by the p90 above.
  Kept for the other five, which makes it half of the answer rather than a
  discarded alternative.

## Consequences

- `assemble.ts` is not dead code. Its box instances stay the 2D plan view — a plan
  states category, not dress — the far LOD, and every archetype without a kit.
- `InstanceBuffer.blockRanges` gains the purpose it was reserved for. The expander
  consumes buildings in the same blockId-then-id order `assemble.ts` packs them
  in, so per-part buffers are block-contiguous too and LOD can swap a block's kit
  ranges for its box range.
- Two comments assert a boundary the code will no longer keep: `assemble.ts`'s
  "the renderer is a dumb consumer that uploads a buffer" and
  `buildingInstances.ts`'s restatement of it. Both narrow when the kit lands — the
  worker owns massing, the kit dresses it.
- Kit assembly waits on asset load and runs on the main thread, unlike the
  worker-side packing it sits beside.
- `.glb` needs `assetsInclude` or a `?url` import: it is absent from Vite 8.1.4's
  `KNOWN_ASSET_TYPES`.
- The reversal is named rather than left open. If a stage ever consumes a floor
  count, `floors` becomes a field on `Building`, additively, and the kit honours it
  instead of deriving it. That is the moment this decision turns over, and nothing
  short of it is.
